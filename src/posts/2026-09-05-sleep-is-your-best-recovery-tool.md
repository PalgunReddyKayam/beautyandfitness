Every goal on this site — stronger, leaner, better skin — depends on sleep more than on any product or programme. It's also the thing most people trade away first.

## What happens when you're short

- **Muscle.** Sleep restriction reduces muscle protein synthesis and raises cortisol. In one study, dieters sleeping 5.5 hours lost 60% more lean mass and less fat than those sleeping 8.5.
- **Appetite.** Short sleep raises ghrelin (hunger) and lowers leptin (fullness). People reliably eat more, and more of it is high-calorie.
- **Skin.** Growth hormone and cell turnover peak during deep sleep. Chronic short sleep is associated with more visible signs of ageing and slower barrier recovery.
- **Training.** Reaction time, coordination and perceived effort all worsen. Injuries in adolescent athletes roughly double under eight hours.

## How much

Most adults do best on seven to nine hours. If you need an alarm every day and feel drowsy in the afternoon, you're probably under.

## What reliably helps

1. **A fixed wake time**, including weekends. It anchors your body clock more than bedtime does.
2. **Morning daylight**, ten minutes outside within an hour of waking.
3. **A cool, dark room** — around 18°C, with as little light as possible.
4. **Caffeine cut-off** roughly eight hours before bed. Its half-life is five to six hours.
5. **Alcohol moved earlier or reduced.** It sedates, then fragments the second half of the night.
6. **Screens down**, not for the blue light so much as for the stimulation.

## On the nights you can't

Don't try to "make it up" with a long lie-in; keep the wake time and take a 20-minute nap before 3 pm if you need it. Train, but lighter — reduce weight or volume rather than skipping. And stop worrying about the occasional bad night; it's the pattern that matters.

## What to do this week

- Pick a wake time and stick to it for seven days.
- Get outside first thing.
- Move your last coffee earlier by an hour.
