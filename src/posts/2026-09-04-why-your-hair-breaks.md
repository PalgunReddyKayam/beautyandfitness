Hair is dead once it leaves the scalp. That sounds bleak, but it's useful: it means no product can "heal" a strand, and it means the goal is simply to damage each strand as little as possible between the day it grows and the day it's cut. Most breakage comes from a handful of habits.

## Where damage comes from

**Heat.** Above roughly 180°C, the proteins in the hair shaft start to change structure and the outer cuticle lifts and cracks. Daily straightening or curling on high heat is the fastest route to breakage.

**Wet handling.** Wet hair stretches up to 30% more than dry hair and is far weaker. Aggressive towel-drying and brushing wet hair from the roots cause a large share of everyday breakage.

**Tension.** Tight ponytails, buns and braids worn daily pull on the follicle and can cause traction alopecia — thinning at the hairline that is reversible early on and permanent if it continues.

**Chemical processing.** Bleach and permanent colour open the cuticle to work, and each round leaves the shaft more porous and fragile.

## What helps

- Turn the heat down. Most tools work well at 150–170°C, and a heat protectant spray measurably reduces damage.
- Squeeze water out with a microfibre towel or old T-shirt rather than rubbing.
- Detangle from the ends upward with a wide-tooth comb, on conditioner-coated hair.
- Alternate tight styles with loose ones, and never sleep in a tight style.
- Wash as often as your scalp needs, not your hair. Oily scalps can be washed daily; dry ones every few days.

## About "repair" products

Conditioners and masks coat the hair, smooth the cuticle and reduce friction. That helps prevent further damage and makes hair look and feel better. Bond-building products can temporarily reinforce some broken bonds in chemically treated hair. None of them restore a strand to its original state — regular trimming is what removes damage.

## What to do this week

- Lower your styling tool by 20°C and see if you notice a difference. You probably won't.
- Swap the towel rub for a squeeze.
- Give your hair two heat-free days.
